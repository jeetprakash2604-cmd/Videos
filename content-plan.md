# Faceless YouTube Content Plan

Niches: Personal Finance · History Mysteries · Psychology & Self-Improvement

## Channel setup (3 separate channels, launched one at a time)

Each topic gets its own channel so YouTube knows exactly who to show it to.

| Channel | Topics | Name ideas | Visual style |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Personal Finance** | Budgeting, saving, investing basics, side income | Money Made Simple · The Smart Wallet · Cash Clarity | Clean stock footage, simple Canva charts and numbers, upbeat lo-fi music |
| **2. Psychology & Self-Improvement** | Human behavior, the mind, habits, emotions, relationships | Mind Decoded · The Quiet Mind · Inner Wiring | Calm stock footage, minimal text animations, soft piano or ambient music |
| **3. History Mysteries** | Unsolved events, lost places, strange history | Lost Archives · Untold Ages · The Vanishing File | Old photos and maps, slow zooms, dark tones, deep narrator voice |

**Launch order (with 1 hour a day):**
- **Month 1:** Psychology only (2 long videos + 6 Shorts a week). It is the easiest to script and grows fast with Shorts.
- **Month 2:** Add History. Each channel gets 1 long video + 3–4 Shorts a week.
- **Month 3:** Add Finance, the highest-paying niche, once you're fast at editing. Do 1 long video every 1–2 weeks per channel plus daily Shorts across all three.

Don't launch all three on day one. One channel posting consistently beats three posting half-heartedly.

## 3 Shorts scripts ready to voice (about 40 seconds each)

### Short 1 (Finance): "Why you're always broke after payday"
> Payday hits... and three days later, it's gone. Here's why.
> Your brain gets a dopamine rush the moment money lands. It feels rich, so it spends like it's rich.
> Psychologists call it the "windfall effect." Money that feels new feels free.
> The fix? Make saving automatic. Set a transfer for the same day your salary arrives, before your brain even sees it.
> You can't spend what you never touch.
> Try it for one month, and tell me in the comments how much you saved.

Footage: [person checking phone happily] [shopping bags] [brain animation] [bank app transfer] [piggy bank]

### Short 2 (Psychology): "The 2-minute rule"
> If you procrastinate on everything, try this.
> It's called the two-minute rule. Don't commit to the whole task. Commit to two minutes.
> Want to read? Read one page. Want to work out? Just put on your shoes.
> Here's the trick: starting is the hardest part. Once you start, your brain wants to finish.
> Two minutes. That's all it takes to beat procrastination.
> Follow for one psychology trick every day.

Footage: [person on couch scrolling] [clock timer] [opening book] [tying running shoes] [person working focused]

### Short 3 (History): "The colony that vanished"
> In 1587, 115 English settlers arrived on Roanoke Island.
> Three years later, their governor returned with supplies... and found no one.
> No bodies. No signs of a fight. Houses taken apart.

> To this day, no one knows for sure what happened to them.
> Some say they joined a nearby tribe. Others think something darker.
> What do you think happened? Comment below.

Footage: [old map of America] [stormy sea ship] [abandoned forest settlement] [carved wood close-up] [foggy forest]

## Prompt to turn any idea into a full script (one prompt, low credits)

```
You are a YouTube scriptwriter for a faceless [personal finance / psychology & self-improvement / history mystery] channel.
Topic: [PASTE IDEA]
Write in one reply:
1. 5 clickable titles (under 60 chars)
2. A 7-minute narration script (~1000 words): hook in first 10 sec, short sentences,
   conversational, one surprising fact or story per section, end with a question for comments
3. In [brackets] after each paragraph: a 3–5 word stock footage search term
4. 3 Shorts scripts (40 sec each) from the best moments
5. Thumbnail text (max 4 words) + description with 5 hashtags
Facts must be accurate; flag anything uncertain.
```

## Rules
- **Finance:** add "This is not financial advice. For education only." to every description. Never promise returns.
- **History:** check key dates and facts on Wikipedia before you publish. Viewers will correct you in the comments.
- Use only royalty-free footage (Pexels, Pixabay) and music (YouTube Audio Library).
- Edit every AI script and add your own opinion or angle so YouTube doesn't flag it as "inauthentic content".

## The Human Algorithm: visual style guide

**Concept:** treat the human mind like a computer. Cognitive biases are "bugs", habits are "code", emotions are "processes" and change is a "system update".

**Look:** dark navy or black backgrounds, neon cyan (#00E5FF) and soft purple (#8B5CF6) accents, monospace font for labels (JetBrains Mono or Space Mono), bold sans-serif captions (Montserrat ExtraBold).

**Mascot:** "The User", a faceless glowing silhouette (outline only, no facial features). Create it once and reuse the same poses (thinking, stressed, walking, sitting, celebrating).

**Mix per video:**
- 50% stock footage, color-graded dark and cool, with a light scan-line or grid overlay
- 30% kinetic text and "system UI" graphics: loading bars, terminal text, popup warnings ("⚠ BUG DETECTED: Spotlight Effect")
- 20% simple mascot scenes (2D, slide and scale motion only)

**Recurring segments:**
- `BUG REPORT`: names a cognitive bias
- `SOURCE CODE`: the science or study behind it
- `PATCH`: the practical fix
- `SYSTEM UPDATE`: outro and call to action

**Tools:** Canva (free animated elements, mascot poses, thumbnails), CapCut (keyframes, glitch transitions, typewriter text, auto-captions), Pexels/Pixabay (footage), YouTube Audio Library (ambient synth music).

**Thumbnails:** dark background, the mascot or a single face from stock, 2–4 words in neon, one warning or code element.
